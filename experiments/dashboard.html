01 · Problem and method

From tracking observations to possession spells

For each frame, predict home possession (H), away possession (A), or dead ball (D). A useful answer must identify both the team and the boundaries of each possession. A frame can be correct while a short turnover is absorbed into a longer spell.

1 · InputsBall and player positions, team membership and visibility at 25 Hz.
2 · Spatial attentionBall, up to 30 player tokens and team context form an unordered set per frame.
3 · Temporal attentionA bidirectional transformer relates frames in a 384-frame window, about 15.4 seconds.
4 · Two outputsH/A/D scores and a probability of a direct team possession change.
5 · DecodeA transition-aware Viterbi decoder combines the outputs into a consistent label sequence.
Why this network?

Attention can combine variable numbers of tracked players without relying on fixed player IDs, then use movement before and after a frame to infer context. N013 uses width 192, three spatial layers, six temporal layers and six attention heads.

What is the tradeoff?

Bidirectional attention and some touch features use future frames, so this implementation is for offline inference. Smoothing reduces spurious changes but can merge short real possessions.

02 · Inputs and hypotheses

What the network sees

These features encode football hypotheses; their presence does not prove that each one independently improves the model. All are computed from tracking positions and visibility, not player identities.

Ball position, movement and visibility

Position and velocity locate play; a visibility flag, last-known position and observation age signal when ball evidence is missing or stale.

Player motion and proximity

Team, position, velocity, distance and movement toward the ball help identify nearby players and distinguish teams competing for control.

Team shape and tempo

Width, length, forward movement, speeds and numbers close to the ball describe collective behavior. Slowing can provide evidence for a stoppage.

Pressure, marking and touch cues

Nearest-opponent distance, marking and ball acceleration/turning aim to reveal contested changes. Touches are inferred candidates, not confirmed events.

Transition supervision

An auxiliary head learns direct H↔A changes from external labels within a ±2-frame radius. Its output changes the decoder's team-switch cost.

Missing tracking

Presence masks indicate absent players; training excludes labels on frames with fewer than four tracked objects to reduce a broadcast-data artifact. Degraded examples also expose the model to missingness.

03 · Reading the evidence

Two metrics answer different questions

SpellF1 · possession segments

Predicted and true possessions are matched one-to-one by temporal overlap; F1 is averaged across overlap thresholds 0.3–0.7. Missing or merging a short spell matters even when many individual frames are correct.

Macro-F1 · frame labels

F1 is computed for each class and averaged, giving home, away and dead equal weight. It describes frame classification, so it does not by itself establish accurate possession boundaries.

The official metric implementation is authoritative. Clean and simulated degraded conditions use the same external audit matches; the five-match validation diagnostics later on this page are a different set.

The evidence bottleneck

In clean validation, 56% of 437 true possessions shorter than two seconds had no frame where the raw model's top class was the correct team. The decoder cannot reliably recover a change for which the network supplies little evidence.

In clean validation, 1,165 spells were predicted against 1,776 true spells; median predicted duration was 8.3 seconds versus 5.0 seconds in the labels. Among true spells, 20.4% were merged into longer predictions. These results point to under-segmentation and missed contested turnovers, though they do not establish how the challenge match will behave.

What to investigate next

Short turnovers

Study ball visibility, player proximity and uncertainty around externally labelled short spells. Improve the evidence for a change before changing decoder penalties.

Robustness and transfer

Compare missing-ball and provider-specific failures on held-out external games. N013's recorded SkillCorner test spellF1 is .452; label definitions and conditions differ across providers.

Separate model contributions

Use single-change ablations and additional seeds to distinguish real gains from variance. Pitch control's audit improvement did not clear the validation gate.

Boundary precision

Evaluate transition precision and recall alongside spell duration. The external validation sensitivity study shows that systematic timing shifts can materially reduce spellF1.

How the research progressed

N008 → N010/N011: player-level pressure, marking and inferred touches targeted missed contested possessions. N010 improved the audit mean; N011 confirmed the configuration with a second seed. N011 → N012/N013: a change head and transition-aware decoder improved the recorded audit mean from .635 to .654/.655. N013 was chosen over N012 on external validation, not because it had the best degraded score.

N014–N017: pitch control, graph relations and a ball-reconstruction task tested whether stronger football structure would help. The combined N014 run deteriorated and its unbounded Gaussian auxiliary loss dominated training. Removing that head (N015) was roughly neutral; pitch control alone (N016) improved the audit mean but failed the validation gate; the bounded ball head (N017) also missed the gate.

E001: averaging two champion seeds produced .658 audit mean, but did not satisfy the full pre-registered criteria. N018 is a larger, longer run: its external validation spellF1 is .5568 and its audit mean is .692. It meets the first-seed numerical gates; second-seed confirmation is still required. A higher audit number alone is insufficient when the agreed validation and confirmation criteria are not met.
